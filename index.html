Alembic

Out now on Steam for Windows, with a free demo.

A falling-sand laboratory built on the real periodic table. Every element carries its real properties, and reactions come from chemistry rather than a recipe book.

Pour elements together. Heat them, cool them, dissolve them, electrify them, compress them, irradiate them, and see what happens.

Get Alembic on Steam Join the Discord

There is no recipe book.

The simulation asks what the materials are, what state they are in, and what you have done to them. What happens next is up to the chemistry.

  • Chemistry you can play with

    Drop sodium into water. Grind aluminium over iron oxide and light the thermite. Plate metals, grow rust, melt sand into glass.

  • Systems built around it

    Pipes, fans, filters and generators work on the same particles, heat and pressure as everything else. Build a steam cycle and turn heat into power.

  • A reactor, or a bomb

    Neutrons scatter, slow and multiply. Keep the chain under control and it boils your coolant. Let it run away and you get something much less microscopic.

  • Your window is a lab flask

    Hold Shift and shake the game window. Powders slam into the walls and liquids slosh. It is not a mini-game. It is the mixing tool.

You fill in the periodic table yourself.

You don't begin with a finished encyclopedia. Experiments fill the Codex as you play, and much of the table opens through discoveries of your own.

Challenges give you something to aim at, from distillation to nuclear destruction. Sandbox mode hands you the whole laboratory at once.

Library at the End of the Universe

Coming soon to Steam for Windows.

You are the curator of the universe's last library, cataloguing history at the end of time. Inscribe runes by hand, bind them into words that bend the rules, and let the library keep writing while you are away.

Wishlist on Steam

Nine Epochs, each ending in a collapse.

An idle incremental about cataloguing a universe as it ends. Play it actively, leave it running, or some of each. Every way of playing reaches the last page.

  1. It starts quiet.

    An empty library, a black hole, and the first rune drawn by hand.

  2. It fills.

    Words bend the rules, and the library keeps writing while you are away.

  3. It collapses.

    And leaves you stronger for the next one.

No micro-transactions. Progress while you are away. A lifetime leaderboard, sealed when you write the last page.

A Cold War submarine auto-battler. Pack a hull with torpedoes, sonar and armour from bow to stern, pick a captain, and engage.

The boats fight it out on their own, yours against another captain's.

The build is the battle.

Between fights you are in the shop, fitting out the boat. Once you engage, it is out of your hands.

  • Bow to stern

    Every part has a shape and has to fit the hull. Where it sits matters: some weapons hit harder from the bow.

  • Find them first

    Weapons mostly miss until your sonar has a lock. Build to get the lock sooner, or to be harder to find.

  • A captain for the build

    Each captain brings a rule of their own. Brooke's weapons fire extra shots while the enemy is burning.

The studio

Exhale Studios is an independent game studio. We build games around systems deep enough to disappear into: a chemistry set that obeys the periodic table, a library at the end of time, a submarine you pack like a suitcase, a dungeon with no bottom floor.

They don't look alike. What they share is how we go about making them.

  • Build the rules, not the moments.

    We would rather make a system that behaves than script a scene that plays once. Alembic has no recipe book. Every reaction comes from what the materials are and what you did to them, so some of what happens surprises us too.

    See it in Alembic

  • Leave things to find.

    We don't hand over a finished encyclopedia on the first screen. What a game holds should open up because you went looking, and the Library keeps its last page for whoever gets there.

    See it in Library at the End of the Universe

  • The thinking is the game.

    Most of what we make is decided before anything moves: the boat you packed, the words you bound, the stance you took going into the fight. We like games that reward a plan more than a reflex.

    See it in Sonar Lock

  • Take the systems seriously, and nothing else.

    A dungeon crawler can have a Bureaucrat who fights with red tape and a Midlife Crisis who throws their back out. The rules underneath still have to hold.

    See it in Disparate Destiny

What comes next

The four games on the front page are where we have started, not the whole list. Some of what we are working on is too early to show. When it is ready, it gets a door.
